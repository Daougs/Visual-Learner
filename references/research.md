# Research protocol

Research is the most important phase. A beautiful video that teaches a wrong number does harm. Spend the effort here.

## 1. Map the subject

- List the core concepts and the order they depend on each other. Draw it as a prerequisite chain: nothing is used before it is taught.
- List what the learner should be able to *do* at the end (calculate X, decide Y, avoid Z). Every lesson serves one of these.
- List the common misconceptions and traps beginners fall into. Each one becomes a warning, a "common mistakes" section or a scenario.
- Find how good teachers and textbooks sequence the subject, and where they disagree.

## 2. Gather facts from primary sources

- Use WebSearch in "extended" mode for anything current, regional, regulatory or numeric. Run several searches in the same turn.
- Open (fetch) every page you take a number, rule or date from. A search snippet is not a source.
- Prefer primary sources: the regulator, the government notice, the official statistics release, the standard's text, the original paper. Use news and explainers only to find them, or when nothing primary exists.
- Record each fact with its URL and an as-of date. The book's Sources section lists the pages actually opened.
- Anything from general knowledge that you can't source is labelled "approximate" in the book.

## 3. Build the worked examples

- Choose a small set of recurring characters or made-up companies and reuse them across lessons, so later chapters can build on earlier numbers (the finance course used one company, "Kolkata Kitchens", for every ratio).
- Compute every example in code (Python) and keep the script; never do multi-step arithmetic by hand.
- Check each result against the source rule a second time, including rounding and add-ons (taxes, fees, cess).

## 4. Review before writing

Spawn a subagent with only the outline and the sources (not your conclusions) and ask: "For a <level> learner aiming to <goal>, what is missing, wrong, out of date or out of order?" Fix what it finds.

## 5. Keep the facts current

Scheduled runs read the book, not the web. Before a lesson that depends on a fast-moving figure (an interest rate, a tax slab, a price), the run checks it again with one extended search and, if it changed, updates the book and notes the change in the Release schedule row.
